Holding Your Thoughts Captive

You don’t have to explain yourself to start, and you don’t have to know what’s wrong. Pick whichever one is closest to true right now.

Use any of these, in any order. Start with grounding and stop there. Walk through the thoughts and skip half the questions. Open it three times in a day or once in a month. There is no right way and nothing is left unfinished.

It works with a paper journal, not instead of one. If you keep one, use these pages as the prompts and write your answers by hand — the questions are the useful part. Every screen can be copied or printed, so you can paste it into whatever you already keep.

Philippians 4:8–9 · ESV Paul, writing from prison, telling a small church what to fill their minds with — a deliberate redirection, not denial that things are hard.

Find one thing to praise Him for

Not because you feel like it. The psalmist didn’t either — he asks his own soul why it’s in turmoil and then says he will praise God anyway. You can be downcast and still sing.

It can be small. The day ends. There is water. Someone texted back. He hasn’t left.

“Why are you cast down, O my soul, and why are you in turmoil within me? Hope in God; for I shall again praise him, my salvation and my God. My soul is cast down within me; therefore I remember you from the land of Jordan and of Hermon, from Mount Mizar.”
Psalm 42:5–6 · ESV Written while displaced from Jerusalem, likely in the region of Mount Hermon — homesick as much as anything else.

Small things, while you sit

Put both feet flat on the floor and let them be heavy.
Unclench your jaw. Drop your shoulders.
Drink some water, even a mouthful.
Name five things you can see from where you’re sitting.
Say one true sentence to God. It can be “I can’t do this.”

Eleven of them

When your inner voice turns on you

You’d probably call this negative self-talk — talking down to yourself, or feeling like your own mind has turned against you. It isn’t random. It comes in patterns, the same handful of them, and each one shows up as an actual sentence in your head. Find the one below that sounds like yours. Naming it isn’t finding a fault in you; nobody thinks straight while they’re hurting.
